The problem

Cities can see today's AQI. They can't act on tomorrow's.

Most public dashboards stop at "here's a number." A city administrator needs more: which station will cross into Poor tomorrow, what's actually causing it, and which intervention to fund first when every ward is asking for the same truck.

01

Passive monitoring

Raw AQI is descriptive, not predictive — by the time it's Severe, the window to act has closed.

02

No source attribution

Knowing the AQI is 400 doesn't say whether to target traffic, construction dust, or biomass burning.

03

Flat prioritization

Sorting by AQI alone misses stations that are trending up fast, or trapped by still weather — until it's too late.

How it works

Four real data feeds, one intervention queue.

Every layer is traceable to a named public source — proxy layers are labeled as proxies, not dressed up as measurements.

Ingest

Live CPCB AQI · Open-Meteo weather · Sentinel-5P (top-20 cities) · OSM land-use

Forecast

Random Forest / XGBoost / HistGB vs. a persistence baseline, time-split evaluated

Attribute

Pollutant-signature classifier cross-validated against land-use, with a confidence score

Prioritize

Multi-factor score — AQI, forecast trend, dispersion, health exposure — ablation-tested against naive AQI sort

Advise

Category-triggered citizen advisories in 7 languages
